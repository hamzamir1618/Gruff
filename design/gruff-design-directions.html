Three ways GRUFF could look online, and one to choose.

Each direction below answers the same brief: a Parisian fries house with the authority of an old newspaper, the warmth of a cinema at night, and the hand of a boutique illustrator. They share a menu and a brand name. They differ in layout, color and mood.

Direction A · Revised

La Gazette

GRUFF as a daily newspaper. The site reads like a front page, and the fries are served in yesterday's edition.

  • LayoutStrict 12-column broadsheet grid with column rules, a masthead, datelines, a photo essay band and classifieds.
  • ColorLight newsprint and black ink. Press red for actions. Real gold leaf on the masthead only.
  • ImageryStock photography: food in full color, people and places in black and white. Public-domain engravings as spot graphics.
  • Feels likeA Paris daily printed this morning, with an archive of old engravings.

Direction C

L'Atelier

GRUFF as a little shop on a Paris street, drawn in ink and watercolor. The homepage is the shopfront; the menu is its window display.

  • ImageryHand-sketched architecture, loose ink lines and organic watercolor shading.
  • Feels likeA patisserie box, or an illustrated travel journal.

Recommendation

Go with La Gazette, as revised

C covers the most of the brief on paper, but the brief names the print grid as the foundation. A gives every page an obvious home, and with stock photography it can launch without commissioned art.

Proposed

GRUFF La Gazette, with one borrowed move

Keep A's newspaper grid, light newsprint palette, stock photography and engraved spot art as the system. Add one element from Direction B where it adds the most.

From B · Le CinémaThe extruded marquee script for the evening "Édition du soir" banner and late-night promotions.
Photography ruleFood in full color, people and places in black and white, so the page always reads as a newspaper.
  • It matches the brief's structure. The brief puts the column grid first and layers depth and sketched elements on top. A+ follows that order.
  • It scales to every page. Menu, locations, story and order flow all map to newspaper sections without new layout ideas.
  • It is the cheapest to launch. Stock photography and public-domain engravings are available now. No 3D studio or painted commissions are needed.
  • It gives the product a story. Frites served in newsprint is a real tradition, so the packaging and the website say the same thing.
